# Interviews

Use this when the domain knowledge comes from people, such as staff, operators or customers, in interviews or chats.

1. **Map the gaps.** Before the first question, make a table of the policies the product needs, with a column each for whom it applies to, deadlines, amounts and limits, exceptions, who approves and who owns it. Link each question to the cell it fills.
2. **Ask who knows more.** End each interview with "Who knows this better than you?", and keep a referral graph with a column for the topics only that person knows.
3. **Go deep on a real case.** For an exception the rulebook does not cover, ask about one real incident in depth: what happened, what they noticed, what they decided and why (the Critical Decision Method).
4. **Confirm what matters twice.** Confirm a rule about money, deadlines or approval with a second person, or mark it as one person's word.
5. **Count what is new.** After each round, count the new facts. When the count falls, change how you ask, such as asking about the topics someone handles instead of asking for a contact.
6. **Turn failures into questions.** For each case that failed in a test, write one question that would explain it, and ask it in the next round.

Write each answer on the board with its source, its date and a confidence label, as the steps in [01-domain.md](01-domain.md) describe; an open answer is a hotspot. Comparing the answers with another model is an option, not a step.
